# 20 Minutes That Could Save Your Teen's Life
### Do this tonight, with your teen, phones and car keys in hand

Every item below is free and already built into equipment you own. Most parents have never turned any of it on. Work top to bottom. Tick each box as you finish.

---

## STEP 1 - THE PHONE (5 minutes, do this first)

Crash detection only works if the phone is on, charged, and with the person. It is your backup when nobody witnesses the crash.

### iPhone
Requires iPhone 14 or newer, iOS 16 or later. Check the model first: Settings > General > About.

- [ ] **Settings > Emergency SOS** - confirm **Call After Severe Crash** is ON
- [ ] **Health app > Medical ID** - tap Edit, add **Emergency Contacts**, blood type, allergies, medications
- [ ] Same screen - turn ON **Show When Locked** and **Share During Emergency Call**
- [ ] **Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services** - ON
- [ ] **Settings > Focus > Driving** - set to activate automatically while driving
- [ ] Older than iPhone 14? Crash detection is not available. Skip to Step 2 and rely on the car and the Apple Watch option below.

### Android
- [ ] Install or open the **Personal Safety** app (built in on Pixel, available on Play Store for others)
- [ ] Turn on **Car Crash Detection** - note this is not available on every model or in every region, so check whether the toggle exists
- [ ] Add **emergency contacts** and **medical information** in the same app
- [ ] Location and Wi-Fi must be on for it to work

### Apple Watch - the single best fix for "the phone was lost"
A watch stays strapped to the wrist in a crash. A phone in a cupholder does not.

- [ ] Series 8, SE 2nd generation, or Ultra and newer, on watchOS 9 or later
- [ ] **Watch app > Emergency SOS** - confirm crash detection is ON
- [ ] If your teen already owns one, make wearing it while driving a house rule

**Know the limits.** Crash detection gives a countdown that a conscious person can cancel. Apple states plainly it cannot detect all crashes. Treat it as a backup, never as protection.

## STEP 2 - THE CAR (10 minutes)

### Tesla (Model 3, Y, S, X)
The most capable teen-driver controls of any car most families own, and almost nobody uses them.

- [ ] Park the car. **Controls > Safety > Parental Controls**
- [ ] Turn on **Limit Speed** and pick a cap
- [ ] Turn on **Reduce Acceleration** (forces Chill mode)
- [ ] Turn on **Require Safety Features**
- [ ] Turn on **Send Curfew Notifications** - you get an app alert whenever the car is shifted into Drive between 11 PM and 4 AM
- [ ] Set a **4-digit PIN**. You set it. Your teen does not get it.
- [ ] In the **Tesla app**, confirm you can see live location and speed
- [ ] Check the touchscreen for an **SOS icon** and check your software release notes for automatic emergency calling. On US cars this may route through a paired phone, so do not assume the car will call on its own.

*Limitation worth knowing: the speed cap floor is 50 mph. It stops highway top-end. It does nothing about 50 in a 25 on a neighborhood street.*

### Ford, GM, Toyota, Honda, Hyundai, Kia and most others
Nearly every major brand has a teen-driver mode. Names differ.

- [ ] Look for **MyKey** (Ford), **Teen Driver** (GM/Chevrolet), or the equivalent in your owner's manual index under "teen"
- [ ] Typical features: speed warning or limit, audio volume cap, seat belt reminder that mutes the radio until belted, forced traction control
- [ ] GM Teen Driver also produces an **in-vehicle report card** showing distance driven, max speed, and safety events
- [ ] Check whether your car has **automatic crash notification** (OnStar, Toyota Safety Connect, Honda Link, Hyundai Blue Link). Many require an active subscription. Confirm yours is current.

### Any car, any age
- [ ] Tires, brakes, wipers, headlights checked. Teach your teen to check them.
- [ ] Phone mount installed, or a rule that the phone goes in the glove box

## STEP 3 - MONITORING (5 minutes)

Pick at least one. Tell your teen you are using it. Covert monitoring costs more trust than the data is worth, and a teen who knows the car reports speed is the entire point.

- [ ] **Your insurer's telematics app** - State Farm Drive Safe & Save, Progressive Snapshot, Allstate Drivewise and others. Free, tracks speed and hard braking, and usually lowers your premium.
- [ ] **Life360** - location plus speed alerts across all drivers and vehicles. Free tier is adequate.
- [ ] **Apple Find My** or **Google Family Link** - location only, no extra app needed
- [ ] Put a **monthly 10-minute review** on the calendar. Look at the data together, calmly. It is a coaching tool, not an interrogation.

---

## STEP 4 - THE CONVERSATION (the part no app replaces)

None of the settings above prevent a crash. These four things do.

- [ ] **Seat belts, every occupant, every trip, back seat included.** In a crash, the person who stays inside the vehicle survives injuries that kill the person who is thrown from it. This is the largest single factor on this page.
- [ ] **Passenger limit.** Virginia allows one passenger under 21 before age 18. Set your household rule at zero non-family passengers for the first several months. Crash risk climbs sharply with each additional teen in the car.
- [ ] **Night.** Virginia bars driving from midnight to 4 AM under 18. Set your household curfew earlier than that.
- [ ] **Unfamiliar roads.** Any route your teen has not driven with you gets driven with you first, in daylight, then at night, before it is driven alone.

**And the one clause that matters most:**

> Call us at any hour from any place if driving or riding is unsafe. No questions, no consequences, no lecture. We will come get you or pay for the ride. This never expires and is never revoked as punishment.

Say it out loud. Then honor it the first time it is used. One angry reaction destroys it permanently.

### The rule nobody knows

Virginia Code § 46.2-334.01(F): **police may not stop a vehicle for violating the passenger or curfew restrictions.** Not a secondary offense - simply not a stopping reason at all.

The state wrote the rules and left the enforcement entirely to you. If you do not enforce them, nobody does.

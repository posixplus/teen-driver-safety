# Tesla Settings for a Teen Driver
### Companion sheet. Park the car, bring the phone, do this in 10 minutes.

Every setting below is standard. **None of it requires a Full Self-Driving subscription.** Menu names shift with software updates - if something is not where this says, search the touchscreen settings for the feature name.

---

## PART 1 - PARENTAL CONTROLS (the only settings your teen cannot undo)

**Controls > Safety > Parental Controls.** Everything else on this sheet can be switched off by whoever is driving. This section cannot, because it sits behind a PIN.

- [ ] **Limit Speed** - ON, pick your cap
- [ ] **Reduce Acceleration** - ON (forces Chill mode)
- [ ] **Require Safety Features** - ON
- [ ] **Send Curfew Notifications** - ON. You get an alert on the Tesla app whenever the car is shifted into Drive between 11 PM and 4 AM.
- [ ] Set a **4-digit PIN**. You set it. Your teen never gets it.
- [ ] Write the PIN somewhere other than your phone

*Limitation: the speed cap floor is 50 mph. It stops highway top-end. It does nothing about 50 in a 25 on a neighborhood street. The curfew alert is the more valuable half of this section.*

---

## PART 2 - LANE AND ROAD DEPARTURE

**Controls > Self-Driving.** These are the settings that address running off the road, which is the single most common fatal teen crash pattern on rural and suburban roads.

- [ ] **Emergency Lane Departure Avoidance** - ON

  Applies steering when the car is departing the road with the turn signal off and a collision may occur. Works between 30 and 90 mph on roads with clearly visible lane markings or curbs.

- [ ] **Lane Departure Avoidance** - set to **Assist**, not Warning

  Warning only vibrates the wheel. **Assist** applies corrective steering if the car drifts toward an adjacent lane or the edge of the road. Works between 40 and 90 mph with clear markings.

**Be realistic about this.** Tesla's own limitations list says these features may fail on narrow or winding roads, on sharp corners at speed, where lane markings are worn, and in poor visibility. A dark rural lane with faded markings is close to the worst case for them. Turn them on for the highway miles your teen will drive for the next ten years. Do not treat them as protection.

---

## PART 3 - COLLISION AND BLIND SPOT

**Controls > Safety**

- [ ] **Automatic Emergency Braking** - ON (usually default). Detects vehicles and obstacles ahead and applies the brakes.
- [ ] **Forward Collision Warning** - leave at default or set to **Early**
- [ ] **Obstacle Aware Acceleration** - ON. Cuts acceleration if an obstacle is detected ahead at low speed. Prevents the pedal-mix-up crashes in parking lots and driveways.
- [ ] **Blind Spot Collision Warning Chime** - ON. Silent visual warnings do not reach a distracted teenager. Make it audible.
- [ ] **Blind Spot Warning Light** - ON

*Automatic Emergency Braking is built for the vehicle in front of you. It is not designed for a tree line approached sideways during a loss of control. Keep it on, but understand what it is and is not for.*

---

## PART 4 - SPEED AWARENESS

**Controls > Safety** (or Autopilot/Self-Driving on some versions)

- [ ] **Speed Limit Warning** - set to **Chime**, not Display

  A number changing color on a screen is invisible to a teenager. A sound is not.

- [ ] Set the offset so it chimes at or just above the posted limit, not 10 mph over
- [ ] **Acceleration Mode** - **Chill**. Parental Controls forces this, but confirm it.

---

## PART 5 - THE TRAP

- [ ] **Check whether your car has Track Mode.** Performance models do.

  Driver assistance features are automatically disabled when Track Mode is on. A teenager can switch off every system on this sheet in about four taps. Know it exists and have the conversation.

- [ ] **Check the touchscreen for an SOS icon.** Automatic emergency calling behavior differs by region and software version, and on US cars it may route through a paired phone rather than the car itself. Do not assume the car will call 911 on its own. Read your release notes.

- [ ] **Tesla app** - confirm you can see live location and speed
- [ ] **Software updates** - keep them current. Safety features improve between versions.

---

## PART 6 - WHAT THE SOFTWARE CANNOT DO

Every setting on this sheet is built to save an attentive adult from one moment of inattention, at highway speed, on a well-marked road. None of it is built to save a teenager driving too fast on a dark unfamiliar lane with friends in the car.

The four things that actually decide the outcome:

- [ ] **Seat belts, every occupant, every trip, back seat included.** In a run-off-road crash, the occupant who stays inside the vehicle survives injuries that kill the occupant who is thrown from it. Largest single factor on this page by a wide margin.
- [ ] **Passenger limit.** Virginia allows one passenger under 21 before age 18. Set your household rule at zero non-family passengers for the first several months.
- [ ] **Night.** Virginia bars driving midnight to 4 AM under 18. Set your household curfew earlier. Use the Tesla curfew alert to verify it.
- [ ] **Unfamiliar roads.** Any route your teen has not driven with you gets driven with you first, in daylight, then at night, before it is driven alone.

> **Call us at any hour from any place if driving or riding is unsafe. No questions, no consequences, no lecture.** We will come get you or pay for the ride. This never expires and is never revoked as punishment.

Say it out loud. Honor it the first time it is used.

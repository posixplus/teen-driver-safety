## Part 1 - The law (not negotiable, not ours to adjust)

These are Virginia's provisional license restrictions, in force until the driver's 18th birthday. Source: Code of Virginia § 46.2-334.01.

**Passengers.** Until age 18, no more than **one passenger under age 21**. After the license has been held one year, up to three passengers under 21 are allowed **only** when driving to or from a school-sponsored activity, when a licensed driver 21 or older is in the front passenger seat, or in an emergency.

The exemption for "family or household" is narrower than most families assume. It covers spouse, children, stepchildren, brothers, sisters, half-siblings, **first cousins**, in-laws living in the same home, and anyone cohabiting with the driver. It does **not** cover the children of family friends, no matter how close the families are. A friend's son riding along counts as a passenger under this law.

**Night driving.** No driving between **midnight and 4:00 a.m.**, except to or from work, to or from an adult-supervised activity sponsored by a school or a civic, religious, or public organization, when accompanied by a parent in the front seat, or in an emergency.

Note that RTS and temple events fall under the civic and religious exemption. That is a legal exemption, not a safety exemption. See Part 2.

**Phone.** Drivers under 18 may not use a phone or any communication device while driving. **Handheld and hands-free are both prohibited.** This is stricter than the rule for adults.

**Seat belts.** Every occupant, every trip, before the car moves.

### The part parents need to understand

Virginia law says **police may not stop a vehicle solely for violating the passenger or curfew rules.** These are secondary offenses. An officer has to have another reason to pull the car over first.

The practical meaning: if your teen breaks these rules, they will most likely not get caught. The rules only work if you enforce them. Nobody else will.

## Part 2 - Our family's rules (stricter than the law)

Check the ones that apply. Discuss each one out loud rather than just signing.

**Night driving**

- [ ] No driving after ______ p.m. for the first ______ months, regardless of the legal midnight limit.
- [ ] Community and temple events run late. Even though the law exempts them, our rule for late events is: ☐ parent drives  ☐ teen leaves by ______ p.m.  ☐ teen calls first

**Passengers**

- [ ] Zero non-family passengers for the first ______ months, even though the law allows one.
- [ ] Cousins count as passengers for our purposes even though the law exempts them. Crash risk does not care about the family tree.
- [ ] Driver tells parent who is in the car, before leaving.

**Unfamiliar roads**

- [ ] Any route the driver has not driven before with a parent is driven **first with a parent**, in daylight, then again at night, before it is driven alone.
- [ ] Applies specifically to: highway merges and interchanges, rural two-lane roads, and any trip outside the immediate Richmond metro area.
- [ ] Trips outside a ______ mile radius require a conversation first.

**Speed**

- [ ] Speed limit is the limit, not a suggestion, including on roads that feel empty.
- [ ] We will use the telematics or driver-monitoring app available through our insurer or vehicle. Reviewed together, monthly, without an argument. It is a coaching tool, not surveillance.
- [ ] App or feature we are using: ________________________________

**Phone**

- [ ] Phone goes in the glove box or bag before the engine starts. Not in a cupholder, not face-down on the seat.
- [ ] Navigation is set before departure. If the route needs changing, pull over.
- [ ] Family group chats and calls from parents are not exceptions. Parents agree not to expect a reply while their child is driving.

**Condition to drive**

- [ ] No driving after fewer than ______ hours of sleep. Late-night studying and early-morning driving is a real crash pattern, not a small thing.
- [ ] Zero alcohol, zero cannabis, zero anything. Virginia's zero tolerance law means any measurable amount is a license loss.
- [ ] Not driving while upset or after a family argument. Wait twenty minutes.

**Weather**

- [ ] First rain drive, first snow or ice drive, and first heavy fog drive are each done with a parent in the car.

## Part 3 - The clause that matters most

**No questions asked, no consequences, no lecture.**

If our child is ever in a situation where driving or riding is unsafe - impaired driver, an impaired friend at the wheel, exhaustion, a car full of people acting recklessly, a route they cannot handle, anything - they can call us at any hour from any place and we will come get them, or pay for a ride, with no argument that night and no punishment afterward.

We will talk about it later, calmly, and the conversation will be about how to avoid the situation, not about blame.

This clause is permanent. It does not expire and it is not revoked as a punishment for anything else in this agreement.

## Part 4 - What parents commit to

An agreement that only binds the teenager is a rulebook, not an agreement.

- [ ] I will complete at least ______ supervised practice hours beyond Virginia's required 45, deliberately covering conditions the required hours usually miss: night, rain, highway merges, unfamiliar routes, and heavy traffic.
- [ ] I will drive the way I expect my child to drive. No speeding, no phone in hand, seat belt every time. They have been watching me drive for sixteen years.
- [ ] I will not call or text my child when I know they are driving.
- [ ] I will not schedule my child so tightly that speeding becomes the only way to be on time.
- [ ] I will keep the car maintained - tires, brakes, lights, wipers - and teach my child to check them.
- [ ] I will react to a mistake or a ticket with a conversation and a restriction, not with rage. Rage teaches them to hide things from me.
- [ ] I learned to drive under different rules and different roads than my child does. I will learn Virginia's teen driving rules properly rather than assuming.

## Part 5 - Consequences

Agreed in advance, so nobody is negotiating in the heat of the moment.

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| First violation of a family rule | Driving privileges paused ______ days |
| Second violation | Driving privileges paused ______ weeks, restart supervised practice |
| Any legal violation (ticket, curfew, passenger rule) | ______ |
| Crash, any severity | Privileges paused pending a full conversation and a plan |
| Hiding a crash, ticket, or violation from parents | Treated more seriously than the original violation |
| Calling for a safe ride under Part 3 | **Nothing. Ever.** |
